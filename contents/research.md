My work sits at the intersection of computer vision and medical imaging. The central question
is how to build models that learn from the kind of data clinical practice actually produces —
limited, weakly annotated, and heterogeneous — and that report their own reliability honestly
enough to be useful to a clinician.

### Research Themes

#### Medical image analysis

Segmentation, classification, and computer-aided diagnosis across breast ultrasound,
breast MRI, mammography, chest X-ray, histopathology, and low-dose CT. Much of this work
targets breast cancer: tumor segmentation, benign/malignant classification, and fine-grained
BI-RADS categorization.

#### Label-efficient learning

Weakly supervised, semi-supervised, and active learning methods that reduce the annotation
burden on radiologists — learning segmentations from image-level labels, generating labels
under information-guided criteria, selecting the most informative images, and augmenting data
to suppress spurious correlations.

#### Trustworthy artificial intelligence

Uncertainty quantification and reliability for medical models: fuzzy uncertainty reduction for
semantic segmentation, adversarial training to improve uncertainty estimates, and fairness
analysis of deep learning diagnosis across race and ethnicity.

#### Deep learning architectures and pattern recognition

Multi-task learning that couples segmentation and classification, attention and transformer
architectures, generative adversarial networks, shape-adaptive convolution, and fuzzy or
neutrosophic formulations of classical pattern recognition models.

#### Large language models and multimodal learning

Applying large language models and vision–language methods to medical imaging, including
radiology report generation, text-guided weakly supervised segmentation, and transformer- and
LLM-based approaches to early cancer detection.

### Research Projects

1. **Breast cancer image analysis** (2016 – present). Semi-supervised and weakly supervised
